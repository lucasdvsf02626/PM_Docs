# UGC Strategy: Zero to First Users

> **Owner:** Lucas Valença · **Status:** Draft v1 · **Market:** UK (supplements)
>
> **Assumptions:** this is a DTC supplement brand selling through Shopify, with no
> customers yet and a small budget. If either is wrong, revisit sections 4 and 8 first.

---

## 1. TL;DR

- At zero users, nobody has made any UGC yet. **You have to create it on purpose**: seed product, pay creators and run a beta cohort.
- The biggest risk here isn't low performance. It's **compliance**. A creator saying "this fixed my anxiety" is an illegal medicinal claim, and the ASA treats it as *your* ad.
- Treat UGC as a **pipeline** (source → brief → review → licence → deploy → measure), not as a one-off batch of videos.
- Spend on **volume of hooks, not polish**. For the first 90 days, aim for ~20 creator assets plus ~30 beta-customer reviews.

---

## 2. What UGC is for at this stage (and what it isn't)

| Job | UGC is good for | UGC is **not** a fix for |
|---|---|---|
| Trust | Real faces and real routines lower the "is this a scam?" barrier on a new brand | A weak offer, poor pricing or a bad product page |
| Paid acquisition | Cheap creative testing on Meta and TikTok, since native-looking ads beat studio ads | A lack of product–market fit |
| Conversion (CRO) | Social proof near the add-to-cart button, plus answering objections (taste, size, routine) | Slow pages or a broken checkout |
| Learning | Comments and reviews show you the customer's own language for copy | Proper user research (do both) |

---

## 3. UGC types and trade-offs

| Type | Cost | Speed | Trust | Usable in ads? | Compliance risk | Use at stage 0? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid UGC creators** (Billo, Insense, TikTok Creator Marketplace, direct) | £50–£250 per video | 1–2 weeks | Medium | Yes, with a licence | Medium: you control the script | ✅ Primary |
| **Gifted micro-influencers** (1k–20k followers) | Product + shipping | 2–4 weeks | High | Only with written rights | High: less control | ✅ Secondary |
| **Beta-cohort reviews** (first 30–50 customers) | Discounted or free product | 4–6 weeks | Highest | Text and photos, yes | Medium: disclosure rules apply | ✅ Essential |
| **Founder content** | Time | Immediate | High for a "small brand" story | Yes | Low | ✅ Cheap filler |
| **Organic customer UGC** | Free | Months | Highest | Needs permission | Low–Medium | ❌ Not yet: wait for customers |
| **AI-generated "UGC" avatars** | Low | Hours | Low, and falling | Risky | **High**: can mislead if presented as a real customer | ⚠️ Only for clearly labelled concept tests, never as testimonials |

**Recommendation:** paid creators for ad creative, a beta cohort for on-site reviews, and founder content for the brand story. Leave everything else until after launch.

## 4. Sourcing playbook (pre-launch)

### 4.1 Beta cohort (reviews and real-world proof)
1. Recruit 30–50 people from your own network, a waitlist landing page and niche communities (gym, running and wellness groups, with each group's rules respected).
2. Give them product at cost or free. **Never make the offer conditional on a positive review** (see section 5).
3. Ask for an honest review at day 14 and day 30 through Shopify review tooling (Judge.me, Okendo or Loox). Use a verified-buyer flag where you can.
4. Ask the best respondents for a photo or a short selfie video, and get written consent and a usage licence (section 6).

### 4.2 Paid creators (ad creative)
- Start with **5 creators × 2 concepts × 2 hooks** = 20 assets.
- Pick creators who **match the buyer**, not the ones with the most followers. A 35-year-old runner selling to 35-year-old runners beats a 22-year-old lifestyle creator.
- Ask for **raw footage and the edit**, so you can re-cut hooks yourself.
- Keep a creator log: name, platform, rate, licence expiry, top-performing asset, and whether you'd use them again.

### 4.3 Micro-influencer seeding
- Send product to 20–30 accounts with a short note. Expect about a 20–30% posting rate.
- An unsolicited gift with no obligation still needs disclosure if they post. Tell them to use **#gifted** or **#ad** (section 5).

## 5. Compliance: the part that has to be right

UK supplements sit under **food law**, not medicines law. Anything that crosses the line becomes an unlicensed medicinal claim. When you pay for or control the content, **the brand is liable for what the creator says**.

### 5.1 Rules that apply

| Rule | What it means for UGC |
|---|---|
| **CAP Code (ASA)**, sections 13 & 15 | Ads must be clearly labelled and claims must be substantiated. Creator content you paid for or influenced is an ad. |
| **GB Nutrition & Health Claims Register** (retained Reg. 1924/2006) | Creators may only use **authorised health claims**, worded close to the approved wording and meeting its conditions of use. Example: "Vitamin D contributes to the normal function of the immune system" is fine; "boosts your immunity" is not. |
| **Medicinal claims ban** (Human Medicines Regs 2012 / MHRA) | No claims to **treat, prevent or cure** any disease or condition. Examples: anxiety, depression, insomnia, IBS, PCOS, COVID, "cured my…". |
| **ASA/CMA influencer guidance** | Put **"#ad" or "Ad"** upfront and visible, not buried in the hashtags. Use "#gifted" only when there's no payment and no control. Using both is safest when unsure. |
| **DMCC Act 2024** (in force since 6 Apr 2025) | **Fake reviews are banned.** So are hidden incentivised reviews, and incentives that depend on the review being positive. You must take reasonable steps to stop fake reviews. Fines can reach 10% of global turnover. |
| **UK GDPR** | Reviewer names, photos and faces are personal data. Get explicit consent, and let people withdraw it. |

### 5.2 Creator dos and don'ts (paste into every brief)

**✅ Say:**
- How it fits your routine, how it tastes, how easy it is, how it looks.
- Authorised claims **verbatim** from the brief's approved list.
- "I've been taking it for X weeks". That's a factual personal experience, with no outcome promised.

**❌ Never say:**
- Any disease or condition name, together with treat, prevent, cure, heal, fix, or "got rid of".
- "Doctor recommended", "clinically proven" or "scientifically proven", unless substantiation is on file and approved.
- Weight-loss amounts or rates (e.g. "lost 5 kg in 2 weeks").
- "Better than [competitor]", or comparisons the brand can't substantiate.
- Anything about pregnancy, children or medication interactions.

### 5.3 Review incentives: allowed vs not allowed

| ✅ Allowed | ❌ Not allowed |
|---|---|
| "Leave an honest review and get 10% off your next order", **given for any review, positive or negative**, and disclosed on the review | "5-star reviews get a free tub" |
| Free product to beta testers, with "Received free product" shown on their review | Hiding or filtering out negative reviews |
| Asking every customer for a review | Staff, friends or family posting reviews as customers |
| Replying publicly to negative reviews | Writing or editing reviews on a customer's behalf |

## 6. Usage rights (licence checklist)

Get it in writing for every asset, even gifted ones. A short e-sign form is enough.

- [ ] **Channels**: organic social, paid social, website, email, marketplaces
- [ ] **Paid usage / whitelisting**: Meta Partnership Ads and TikTok Spark Ads run from the creator's handle
- [ ] **Duration**: 6 or 12 months, or perpetual. Perpetual costs more, but it saves takedown pain later.
- [ ] **Territory**: UK, or worldwide if you plan to expand
- [ ] **Edits allowed**: re-cutting, captions, hooks, music
- [ ] **Exclusivity**, if any: e.g. no competing supplement brand for 90 days
- [ ] **Consent** to show face and name, and how to withdraw it
- [ ] **Creator is 18+**: don't use under-18s for supplements

Track licence expiry dates in the creator log, and **pull ads before a licence expires**.

## 7. Brief template (one page per concept)

```
Product:           [name, format, key differentiator]
Audience:          [who they are, the moment they'd use it]
Concept:           [e.g. "morning routine", "unboxing + first taste", "3 reasons I switched"]
Hook options (pick 2, first 3 seconds):
  1. [pattern interrupt / question / visual]
  2. [...]
Must show:         [product on screen within 3s, label legible, in-use moment]
Approved claims:   [verbatim from GB NHC Register; nothing else]
Banned words:      [section 5.2 list]
Disclosure:        "Ad" in first line of caption + platform paid-partnership toggle
CTA:               [e.g. "Link in bio, 20% off first order"]
Specs:             9:16, 15–45s, natural light, no background music (we add), raw files + edit
Deliverables:      2 videos × 2 hooks, 3 stills, raw footage
Deadline / rate / licence: [...]
```

## 8. Review and approval workflow

```
Brief sent → Draft received → Compliance check → Brand check → Licence signed → Live → Tagged in tracker
                                    ↑ fail = reshoot or cut
```

**Compliance check (blocking, before anything goes live):**
1. Every claim is on the approved list, or the asset is cut or reshot.
2. No disease or condition words in the voiceover, captions or on-screen text.
3. Disclosure is visible in the first line, and the platform's paid-partnership label is on.
4. A licence is signed and on file.

Keep a record of **who approved what, and when**. That's your "reasonable steps" evidence under the DMCC Act and your defence if the ASA asks.

## 9. Where UGC goes (CRO placement)

| Placement | What to show | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Product page, above the fold** | Star rating and review count next to the price, linked to the reviews | The biggest trust signal, right at the buying decision |
| **Product page, near the add-to-cart button** | A short UGC video (muted autoplay, captions) or 3 review snippets | Answers "does this actually work for people like me?" before they click |
| **Product page, below the fold** | Full reviews with photos, filterable by goal or format | Lets serious buyers dig deeper without cluttering the top |
| **Landing pages (paid traffic)** | The same creator as the ad that brought them | Keeps the message consistent from ad to page and stops people bouncing |
| **Email/SMS (post-purchase, abandoned cart)** | One review and one UGC still | Cheap reminder of social proof |
| **Ads** | Raw creator video with your own hook re-cuts | The main use for paid creator content |

**Avoid:** a UGC carousel at the very top of the page that pushes the price and the add-to-cart button below the fold on mobile. That's a common way to lose conversions.

### First A/B tests (once there's traffic: aim for ~1k sessions per variant)
1. A UGC video near the add-to-cart button **vs** a static lifestyle image.
2. A review snippet under the price **vs** none.
3. Hook A **vs** hook B on the same creator body. This is the cheapest test, so run it in ads first.

## 10. Metrics

| Stage | Metric | Early target (benchmark, adjust once you have data) |
|---|---|---|
| Ad creative | Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) | >25% |
| Ad creative | Hold rate (15-second views ÷ 3-second views) | >30% |
| Ad creative | CTR (link) | >1% |
| Ad creative | CPA vs. target CPA | Kill if >1.5× target after ~£50 spend |
| On-site | Conversion rate on product pages with UGC vs without | A rise you can see, confirmed by an A/B test |
| Reviews | Number of reviews on the hero product | 30+ before you scale paid spend |
| Reviews | Average rating | Show everything honestly. A 4.3–4.7 average looks more believable than a perfect 5.0. |
| Pipeline | Asset cost and asset win rate | Track for each creator to decide who to rebook |
| Compliance | Assets rejected at review, and the reasons | Trend towards 0 as briefs improve |

## 11. 30 / 60 / 90 plan

| Phase | Deliverables |
|---|---|
| **Days 0–30** | Approved-claims list signed off · brief template and banned-words list · licence form · creator log · beta cohort recruited and shipped · 5 creators booked |
| **Days 31–60** | 20 creator assets through review · first 15–30 beta reviews live on the product page · first ad tests (hooks) · reviews app live with verified badges |
| **Days 61–90** | Rebook the top 2 creators · whitelist the best creator for Spark or Partnership Ads · first on-site A/B test · post-purchase review flow automated · retro on what to keep and what to cut |

## 12. Open questions

- Which products and formats are first to market? That decides which authorised claims are available.
- Do we have substantiation on file for any claims beyond the GB NHC register (e.g. ingredient studies)?
- What's the budget split between creator fees and media spend? Suggested starting point: about 30% creative, 70% media.
- Which reviews app are we using, and does it support incentive disclosure tags?
